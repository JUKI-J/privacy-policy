IntervalRunner Privacy Policy

Effective Date: October 3, 2026

IntervalRunner ("the App", "we", "our") is developed and operated by JUKI, an individual developer in the Republic of Korea (business registration number 604-20-14584). Contact: support@setlog.net.

IntervalRunner is an interval running app. You set time, distance, or heart-rate intervals, and the App alerts you by voice, sound, and haptics or vibration when you drift from your target pace. This Privacy Policy covers every version of the App: the app for iPhone and Apple Watch (iOS and watchOS) and the app for Android phones and Wear OS watches. Where the versions work differently, the section says which version it describes. In every version, the App has no user accounts, and we do not operate a server for it.

In short: in every version, your health, location, and running data are stored on your devices. The App does not send them to us or to any server of ours, and they are never sold or used for advertising. They leave your device only through features you control: files you export or choose to send us (Sections 11 and 12), your device backups (Section 10), watch sync (Section 9), and maps, which load map images for the area shown (Section 5).

iPhone and Apple Watch: the developer does not collect your data through the App. The only exceptions are a message or a diagnostics file you choose to send us (Sections 12 and 20). Your health, location, and workout data stay on your device and, if you allow it, are written to the Health app. The App does not share them with third parties. This version has no advertising and no analytics or tracking SDKs.

Android and Wear OS: the Free version shows ads: Google AdMob provides them using your device's advertising ID (which you can reset or delete) and app set ID, your IP address (from which a general location can be estimated), how you interact with ads, and diagnostics from the ad SDK. Where the law requires it, you are asked for consent first. Health, location, and running data are never used for ads and are never passed to the ad SDK. While you have Pro, there are no ads and the ad SDK is not started. If the birth year you enter shows you are under 18, the App shows no ads. Google's map code runs only when you open a run's map; map images for that area are then loaded from Google.

3. Health Data

iPhone and Apple Watch: HealthKit

  • Written to Health (with your permission): workouts (outdoor or indoor running, including the interval segments), distance, active energy, the workout route, and heart-rate readings recorded during a workout — including those from a Bluetooth heart-rate monitor you connect to your iPhone.
  • Read from Health (with your permission): heart rate, resting heart rate, date of birth, and running speed.
  • Why: to record your run in the Health app, to measure heart rate during a run, and to suggest heart-rate zones and estimate calories. The App does not request or read any other Health data.
  • Where it goes: HealthKit data is kept on your device and in your Health database. The App does not upload it to us or to any server of ours and does not store it in iCloud. Heart-rate readings from your workouts can be part of a diagnostics file only if you choose to export one and send it to us (Section 12). How the Health app itself stores and syncs your data (for example, through iCloud) is managed by Apple and your own settings.
  • How it is not used: HealthKit data is never used for advertising, marketing, or data mining, is never sold, and is never disclosed to advertisers, data brokers, or any other third party. It is not used to promote the subscription.
  • Optional: if you deny read access, the App still works; heart-rate zones and calories that depend on that data are simply not filled in automatically. You can review or revoke access in the Health app (Sharing → Apps) or in Settings.

Android and Wear OS: Health Connect

Health Connect is Android's on-device store for health and fitness data. It is built into Android 14 and later; on Android 9 to 13 it is an app from Google Play. The App uses Health Connect only to keep a record of the runs you do with the App and to suggest heart-rate zones and calorie estimates. Running works without Health Connect.

What the App writes (with your permission). When you finish a run, the App writes:

  • Exercise session: running or treadmill running, with the interval segments, pauses, and laps. The session title is the name you gave the run or the workout's name; other apps with access to your Health Connect data can see this title. Your personal notes are not written.
  • Exercise route: the route of outdoor runs, only if you allow route access.
  • Distance and active calories burned. For runs recorded on the phone alone, calories are estimated from distance and your body weight; if no weight is available, no calories are written.
  • Heart rate measured during the run by your Wear OS watch or a Bluetooth heart-rate monitor.

What the App reads (with your permission):

  • Weight — your latest weight, used only to estimate the active energy (calories) of the runs you save.
  • Resting heart rate — your latest value, to suggest heart-rate zones.

Nothing else is read. The App does not request access to your Health Connect history (so it only sees data from about 30 days before you first granted access onward) and does not read Health Connect data in the background. The App may read back the records it wrote itself, to avoid duplicates and to delete them when you ask. Health Connect does not provide your age; if you want age-based heart-rate zones, you can enter your birth year in the App instead (Section 4).

How Health Connect data is used and not used:

  • It is stored on your device. The App does not send it to us or to any server of ours.
  • It is never used for advertising or marketing, and it is never passed to the ad SDK or any other third-party SDK.
  • It is never sold, and it is never given to data brokers, advertising platforms, insurers, lenders, or employers, or used for decisions about credit, insurance, or employment.
  • The App does not transfer Health Connect data to anyone. The run data the App keeps itself (including heart rate measured during runs) leaves your device only as described in Sections 9 to 12 — watch sync, your own backup, files you export, and a diagnostics file you choose to send us.
  • We have no access to your data in Health Connect.

The use of information received from Health Connect will adhere to the Health Connect Permissions policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your control:

  • Grant or revoke each permission at any time in Health Connect (in Android Settings on Android 14 and later, or in the Health Connect app on Android 13 and earlier). The App keeps working without it.
  • When you delete a run in the App, or delete all data, you can choose to also delete the records the App wrote to Health Connect. Only records the App created are deleted.
  • Records already written to Health Connect stay there after you uninstall the App, until you delete them in Health Connect.

4. Data the App Uses on Your Devices

Everything in this section is processed on your phone or watch only. Each permission is requested when it is first needed, and you can change it at any time in your device's Settings; Settings › Data & Privacy in the App shows their status.

Location ("While Using the App" only)

  • What: your position during an outdoor workout.
  • Why: to calculate your pace and distance and to record your route.
  • Limits: the App never asks for "Always" (background) location access and does not use location when no workout is running. Treadmill mode does not use location at all.
  • iPhone and Apple Watch: location keeps working with the screen off while a workout is active, and iOS shows its usual location indicator. It is stored with the workout on your device and, if Health access is granted, in the workout route in Health. The App does not send it to us.
  • Android and Wear OS: your precise position comes from Android's location services (on the phone, Google Play services' location provider; on a Wear OS watch, the watch's location services). While a run is active, the App runs a foreground service with an ongoing notification so tracking continues with the screen off. Your position is stored with the run on your device and, if you allow it, written to Health Connect as the exercise route. The App does not send it to us or give it to the ad SDK, and the map does not receive your position (Section 5). How Google's location services on your device handle location (for example, the "Google Location Accuracy" setting) is governed by Google and your device settings.
  • Routes and coordinates leave your device only when you export or share them yourself (Section 11).

Motion & Fitness / Physical Activity

  • What: step count and cadence from the device's motion sensors ("Motion & Fitness" on iPhone, the "Physical activity" permission on Android).
  • Why: to refine GPS pace, detect stopping and starting, and show a reference pace on the treadmill.

Bluetooth

  • What: a connection to a Bluetooth heart-rate monitor you choose (on Android, the "Nearby devices" permission).
  • Why: to read your heart rate during a run. The App looks only for heart-rate monitors, does not connect to anything else, and does not use Bluetooth for tracking or to determine your location.

Wear OS watch sensors (Android and Wear OS)

  • On a Wear OS watch, the App uses the watch's heart-rate sensor (body sensors / heart-rate permission), GPS, and motion sensors through Wear OS Health Services during a run, for the same purposes as above.

Information you enter (Android and Wear OS)

  • Workout names, run titles, notes, maximum and resting heart rate, heart-rate zones, and preferences you enter are stored on your device.
  • Birth year (optional): if you want age-based heart-rate zones, you can enter your birth year in the App (Health Connect does not provide it; on iPhone, the App reads your date of birth from Health instead, Section 3). Only the year is stored, and the youngest age you can enter is 13. It is used only to compute your maximum heart rate and zones, and — if it shows you are under 18 — to turn ads off on that device (Section 6). It is stored only on this device, in storage that is excluded from Android backup and from device-to-device transfer. The App does not transmit it — not to us, to Google, to the ad SDK, or anywhere else — and does not include it in diagnostics files (a diagnostics file shows only whether ads are switched off because of age, never the year or your age). Delete All Data deletes it. After restoring a backup or moving to a new device, you enter it again if you want.

Notifications

  • Local notifications scheduled on your device, for example a reminder before a free trial ends or when a workout has been paused for a long time. There are no push notifications and no marketing messages.
  • Android and Wear OS also show an ongoing notification while a run is active (required by Android for the foreground service) and a notice if an unfinished run can be recovered.

Voice guidance (AI-generated voice)

  • Spoken alerts are pre-made voice clips (AI-generated speech) bundled with the App and played on your device. No speech is generated or downloaded while you use the App. The voice is not the voice of a real person. The App does not use the microphone or record audio.

5. Route Maps

iPhone and Apple Watch: Apple Maps (MapKit)

  • The run detail screen shows your route on a map drawn with Apple's MapKit; a shared image includes a map only if you switch it on (Section 11).
  • The route line is drawn on your device and is not sent to us. To display the map, MapKit loads map images for the area being shown from Apple. Apple's handling of this is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Android and Wear OS: Google Maps

  • The run detail screen shows your route on a map provided by the Google Maps SDK for Android.
  • The route line and start/finish dots are drawn on your device. The App does not send your route or coordinates to Google, and it does not enable the map's "my location" feature.
  • To show the map, the Google Maps SDK downloads map images (tiles) for the area being displayed from Google's servers. These requests reveal to Google the general area of the map you are viewing (around your route), together with your IP address. According to Google, the SDK also collects device information (such as OS version and model), SDK version, crash and diagnostic information, and a Maps-SDK-specific pseudonymous identifier, and may record map camera (pan/zoom) events. This happens whether you use Free or Pro.
  • The App does not start Google's map code when it launches; the Google Maps SDK is loaded and contacts Google only when a run's map is shown.
  • Use of the map is subject to the Google Maps/Google Earth Additional Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Shared run images (Section 11) do not use Google map tiles.

6. Advertising

iPhone and Apple Watch: no ads

The iPhone and Apple Watch app shows no ads, contains no advertising SDK, does not access the advertising identifier (IDFA), and does not track you across other apps or websites.

Android and Wear OS: ads in the Free version

The Free version shows ads so that everything you need to run can stay free. Ads are provided by Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK), and consent is managed with Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). Ads appear only on the Workouts (home) and History tabs (a banner), after you close a run summary (occasionally and limited in frequency — currently at most once a day — full screen), and when you choose to watch a video ad to unlock run analysis for 24 hours. Ads are never shown, loaded, or requested during a run.

What the ad SDK collects (Google's disclosure):

  • Device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and the app set ID.
  • IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location.
  • Interactions: app launches, ad views, taps, and video views.
  • Diagnostics: performance information about the App and the SDK (such as launch time, hang rate, and energy use).

This information is sent by the SDK directly to Google, encrypted in transit, and is used by Google for showing and measuring ads, analytics, and fraud prevention. We do not receive or store it. Google's use is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and the Google Privacy Policy; Google's ad partners are listed at support.google.com/admob/answer/9012903.

What we never give the ad SDK:

  • No health, heart-rate, fitness, workout, or run data, and no location from the App's location permission. Ad requests contain no keywords, content, or targeting information. The ad module is built so it cannot access the App's health, workout, or location data: to keep ads out of runs and limit how often they appear, it is told only whether a run is in progress, that a run was completed and how long it was, and whether the user is under 18 (yes/no, never the age) — and it passes none of this to the ad SDK.
  • The App opts out of Android's Privacy Sandbox Topics and Attribution Reporting APIs (it removes their permissions), so the ad SDK cannot use them to derive interest topics from your use of the App.
  • Ad content is limited to a "Teen" maximum content rating, and ads play muted.

Consent and your choices, by region:

  • EEA, UK, and Switzerland: before any ad is requested, Google's consent message (IAB TCF) asks whether you agree to personalized ads and related data use. If you do not consent, you get only limited, non-personalized ads or no ads, and every feature of the App still works. You can change your choice at any time in the App: Settings › Data & Privacy › Ad privacy.
  • United States: where US state privacy laws apply, you can opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal information for targeted advertising in Settings › Data & Privacy › Ad privacy (shown when required in your state).
  • Republic of Korea: the advertising ID is used for ads as described above. You can refuse personalized (behavioral) advertising at any time by deleting or resetting the advertising ID or turning off ad personalization in Android Settings (see below).
  • Everywhere: in Android Settings › Privacy › Ads (on some devices Settings › Google › Ads) you can reset or delete your advertising ID; after deleting it, apps receive no advertising ID. Settings › Data & Privacy › Ad privacy in the App links there. You can also manage personalization at adssettings.google.com.

The consent choice is stored on your device by Google's UMP SDK; the App does not keep its own copy. The consent choice and the ad SDK's other on-device data are excluded from Android backup and device-to-device transfer, so on a restored or new device you are asked again. Uninstalling the App or deleting its data does not delete information Google has already received; to make requests about that information, use the options in the Google Privacy Policy.

Pro has no ads. While IntervalRunner Pro is active (including a free trial and Google Play's grace period), the App shows no ads and does not start the Google Mobile Ads SDK, so the SDK does not send ad requests from your device. If you upgrade while the App is open and the SDK was already started in that session, ads stop and are discarded immediately, and the SDK is not started from the next time the App starts. If Pro ends, ads return from the next time the App starts.

Users under 18. If the birth year you enter (Section 4) shows you may be under 18 (because the App stores only the year, this includes the year in which you turn 18), the App shows no ads on that device and does not start the ad SDK or the consent form from then on (if the SDK was already started in that session, ads stop immediately and the SDK is not started from the next time the App starts). The ad module only learns "under 18: yes/no", never your age or birth year.

On-device ad counters. To limit how often ads appear, the App keeps on your device the install time, the number of completed runs, when ads were last shown, and when a 24-hour analysis pass expires. These counters are not backed up and are never sent automatically; they are part of a diagnostics file only if you export one and choose to send it (Section 12).

8. Data Stored on Your Device

The following stays on your device and is never uploaded to any server of ours:

  • Your saved workouts, presets, alert profiles, and settings (on Android, also folders)
  • Workout history: summaries, step-by-step results, heart-rate and pace samples, and routes (on Android, also alert events, and per-second samples of pace, distance, heart rate, cadence, and GPS position for outdoor runs)
  • Local counters used to improve the App's design, such as how many times a screen was shown (on Android, also the ad counters in Section 6); they are never sent anywhere automatically (see Section 12)
  • Engine traces of your recent workouts, used only for the diagnostics file: speeds, distances, heart rate, step states, and alert events, with positions only in a private coordinate frame (Section 12); and a temporary recovery point for a workout in progress
  • iPhone: a cached copy of your subscription status: whether Pro is active, the kind of subscription (trial, monthly, or yearly), when it expires, and when it was last checked with Apple. It contains no payment or Apple Account details. It is kept in the App's own storage and in the iOS Keychain; iOS may keep Keychain items even after the App is uninstalled
  • Android: a size-limited log of recent app events, the cached subscription status (Section 7), and a random installation identifier used only to sync between your own phone and watch

iPhone and Apple Watch: the App's widgets and Live Activities read a small amount of the same data (for example the current step and remaining time) on your device.

Android and Wear OS: the App's home-screen widgets, the ongoing run notification, and Wear OS tiles and complications show a small part of this data on your devices.

9. Watch Sync

Apple Watch: if you use Apple Watch, the App exchanges workouts, settings, and live workout state between your iPhone and your own paired Watch using Apple's WatchConnectivity and HealthKit. This traffic goes directly between your devices.

Wear OS: if you use the Wear OS app, the phone and watch exchange workouts, settings, live run status, finished runs, and subscription status using Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer. This data goes only between your own phone and watch running IntervalRunner. When they are not connected directly over Bluetooth, Google Play services may relay it through Google's servers; Google states that data relayed this way is end-to-end encrypted. We do not receive it. Runs recorded on the watch are synced to the phone, and the phone writes them to Health Connect if you allow it.

10. Backups and Moving to a New Device

iPhone and Apple Watch

IntervalRunner does not use iCloud or CloudKit to sync data. As with other app data, these files may be included in the iCloud or computer backups you make of your device, which are governed by Apple's terms and your own backup settings.

Android and Wear OS

  • Google cloud backup: if Android backup is turned on, Android's Auto Backup may save part of the App's data to your Google account: your saved workouts, folders, presets, alert profiles, and settings; app state such as the home layout, recent workouts, which notices you have seen, and the on-device usage counters (Section 8); and your run history summaries (times, distances, paces, heart-rate summaries, step results, and alert events). Google encrypts these backups, using your device's screen lock where available. This is governed by Google's terms and your backup settings; you can turn backup off in Android Settings.
  • Excluded from cloud backup: your birth year, per-second samples including GPS routes, engine traces and diagnostics files, the App's log, run recovery data, the subscription status copy, the sync identifier, ad counters, and data stored on the device by Google's ad and consent SDKs (including your consent choice and the ad WebView's data). After restoring from a cloud backup, your runs and summaries are there, but their routes and charts are not.
  • Device-to-device transfer: when you copy data directly from an old Android device to a new one (cable or local transfer, Android 12+), the App's run data including routes may also be copied; the birth year, subscription status, sync identifier, ad counters, and the ad and consent SDKs' data are not.
  • The Wear OS app does not use cloud backup; watch runs are backed up as part of the phone's data after they sync.

11. Exports and Sharing You Start

Apart from watch sync (Section 9), your own backups (Section 10), and map images loaded for the area shown (Section 5), data leaves your device through the App only when you choose to export or share it: GPX route files, CSV or JSON exports of your history, workout files, and run images. Files you share go wherever you send them, under that destination's terms.

  • iPhone: the shared image does not include a map unless you switch it on; when it does, the route's first and last 300 m are left out and the map is blurred within about 300 m of the start and the finish.
  • Android: the share image does not contain a map; when you include the route, its first and last 300 m are left out.

12. Diagnostics (Only If You Choose)

If something goes wrong, you can export a diagnostics file from Settings › Data & Privacy. Nothing is sent automatically. The file is created on your device and leaves it only if you pick a destination in the share sheet (for example, emailing it to us).

  • What it contains (iPhone): device model, iOS version, app version, language and region setting, time zone, Low Power Mode state, the status of each permission, audio output history (output types such as speaker or headphones, never device names), the local counters described above, and the engine traces of your most recent workouts on the iPhone, together with any traces your Watch has sent to it (speeds, distances, heart-rate readings, step states, and alert events). In the iPhone version the heart-rate readings are always part of these traces. A file exported from the App's hidden developer tools may also contain the App's own log lines from the last 24 hours, with coordinate-like text removed.
  • What it contains (Android): device model, Android version, app version, language and region, time zone, battery saver state, the status of each permission, audio output types (such as speaker or headphones — never device names), the on-device counters described above, recent app exit reasons recorded by Android (with coordinate-like text removed), the ad module's state and recent ad events (whether ads are on or switched off and why — for example Pro or the age setting — the ad counters described in Section 6, and when an ad slot was shown, skipped, or failed; no advertising ID or other identifiers), and engine traces of your most recent runs on the phone together with traces your watch sent to it (speeds, distances, step states, alert events, and the workout's settings, including any heart-rate targets you set). Heart-rate readings are left out of the traces unless you switch on "Include heart rate" on the export screen (off by default; it applies only to that export). A file exported from the App's hidden developer tools may also contain the App's own log lines from the last 24 hours, with coordinate-like text removed.
  • What it does not contain: no latitude or longitude. Positions in traces are converted to a private coordinate frame with a random origin and rotation for each session, so that real places are hard to identify, and any trace that contains latitude or longitude fields is left out. No name, email, advertising ID, user-chosen device names, workout history, or Health or Health Connect data values other than the heart-rate readings in the traces described above.
  • Crash reports (iPhone and Apple Watch): the App has no crash-reporting SDK. If you have chosen to share analytics with developers in iOS Settings, Apple may provide us with anonymous crash information through Xcode Organizer and App Store Connect; we do not receive anything that identifies you.
  • Crash reports (Android and Wear OS): the App has no crash-reporting or analytics SDK of its own. If you have chosen to share usage and diagnostics data with Google on your device, Google Play may provide us with anonymous crash and performance statistics (Android vitals); we cannot identify you from them.

Heart-rate readings are health information. A diagnostics file that contains them reaches us only if you choose to send it. By sending such a file, you consent to our using the heart-rate readings in it only to answer you and fix the problem. You can leave them out (on Android, keep "Include heart rate" off), and you can ask us at any time to delete a file you sent.

If you send us a diagnostics file or a message, we use it only to answer you and fix problems, and we delete it as described in Section 15.

15. Retention and Deletion

iPhone and Apple Watch

  • Permissions: you can review or change Location, Health, Motion & Fitness, Notifications, and Bluetooth at any time in the iOS Settings app; Settings → Data & Privacy in the App shows their current status and links there.
  • Delete a workout or run: from the history screens in the App.
  • Delete all data: Settings → Data & Privacy → Delete All Data removes the App's workouts, history, settings, alert profiles, traces, diagnostics files, temporary export files, and widget data on the iPhone. It also clears the Keychain copy of your subscription status; the App then re-reads the status from Apple, so an active subscription stays active. It tells the paired Watch to do the same, and the Watch applies this the next time the Watch app runs; workouts recorded on the Watch after you deleted are kept. Health data is handled separately: the delete dialog has its own "Also delete from Health" switch (off by default) that removes the workouts the App saved to Health, and it only deletes the ones it created. Everything else in Health remains under your control in the Health app.
  • Uninstall: removing the App removes its data from that device, except the cached subscription status in the iOS Keychain, which iOS may keep and which the App reuses if you install it again (use Delete All Data before uninstalling to clear it). Workouts already saved to Health stay in Health until you delete them there.
  • Subscription: the subscription itself is managed by Apple; the App keeps only the cached status described above.

Android and Wear OS

  • Delete a run: from the history screens, optionally also deleting its Health Connect records.
  • Delete all data: Settings › Data & Privacy › Delete All Data removes the App's workouts, history, samples, settings, birth year, alert profiles, traces, logs, diagnostics files, temporary export files, and widget data on the phone, and compacts the database so deleted entries are not left behind. It tells your watch to do the same the next time the watch app runs. An option in the same dialog also deletes the records the App wrote to Health Connect (only those). The subscription status copy and the sync identifier are kept, so an active subscription stays active.
  • Uninstall: removes all of the App's data from that device. Records in Health Connect stay until you delete them there. A copy in your Google backup is managed by Google and your backup settings.
  • Data held by Google (ads, maps, purchases) is retained under Google's policies.

Messages you send us (all versions)

  • Support messages and diagnostics files you send us are deleted within 90 days after your inquiry is resolved.
  • Messages about payments, refunds, withdrawal of purchase, or disputes (and order information we used to handle them) are kept for 3 years as records of consumer complaints and dispute handling, as required by Korea's Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and then deleted.
  • When a retention period ends, we delete the contact-form entry together with the email and any attachments.
